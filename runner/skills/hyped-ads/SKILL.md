# Hyped ads

Read ../hyped/SKILL.md first, then the format guide for the job's format (../hyped/formats/<format>.md; most ads are
vertical). Then read the recipe for the job's template (see Recipes below). The job prompt wins over all of them.

## What an ad must do

An ad plays in a feed, on a phone, usually muted, to someone who didn't ask for it and is about to swipe. In 15-30
seconds it has four jobs, in this order:

1. **Stop the thumb** in the first second: a pain they recognise or a result they want, big, already on screen.
2. **Make one pain concrete**: one situation, shown. Not a list of problems.
3. **Show the product ending it**: the real UI doing the one thing that removes that pain.
4. **Leave a place to go**: the name, the logo and the web address, held long enough to remember.

One pain, one promise, one product moment. If a feature doesn't serve that pain, it isn't in the ad.

| | Ads (`hyped-ads`) | Launch (`hyped-launch`) | Explain (`hyped-explain`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| The viewer | a stranger, scrolling | someone following the product | someone who chose to learn |
| Opens on | the pain, or the result | the product's best moment | the goal |
| Covers | one pain, one fix | what's new and why it matters | one workflow, step by step |
| Pace | a change every 1.5-2 s | about every 3 s | one step every 5-20 s |
| Sound | must work muted | music, often a voice | usually a voiceover |

## Story shapes

The job prompt gives the template's beats already scaled to the length; these shapes say what fits in them. With no
template, use the Problem → Solution shape.

| Length | Beats (seconds) | Scenes |
|---|---|---|
| 15 s | Pain 0-1.5 · Cost 1.5-4.5 · Fix 4.5-10 (two product moments) · Proof 10-13 · CTA 13-15 | 5 |
| 20 s | Pain 0-2 · Cost 2-6 · Fix 6-13 · Proof 13-17 · CTA 17-20 | 6 |
| 30 s | Pain 0-3 · Cost 3-9 · Fix 9-20 · Proof 20-26 · CTA 26-30 | 8 |
| 60 s | Pain 0-6 · Cost 6-18 · Fix 18-40 (the same fix in four or five moments) · Proof 40-52 · CTA 52-60 | 14-16 |
| 90 s | Too long for a feed. If the job asks, keep the 60 s shape and add a second situation with the same pain, fixed the same way | 20+ |

- A scene lasts 2-4 s, and inside it something changes every 1.5-2 s: a word lands, the camera moves, the cursor
  clicks, a result appears.
- The product's name and logo are on screen by second 5 in every ad: after the hook, never before it.
- The core hook rule holds even when a template opens on the pain or the old way: the product's real UI or result is
  on screen by second 3. The recipes show how: a product peek with the logo at the end of the Pain beat, or a split
  frame 0 in Before / After.
- The end card holds 2-3 s, settled (the format guide's end card). Feeds loop, and the last frame is what's on
  screen when the viewer decides.
- At 60 s and over the job prompt asks for 3-5 features. In an ad they're moments of the same fix, all answering the
  one pain, not a tour.

## Hook formulas

Pick the formula that fits the product's own message. Every example below is an invented product.

| Formula | Example | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Name the moment | "Sunday, 11 pm. Rota still blank." (Shiftnest, staff rotas for cafés) | the pain happens at a time or place everyone knows |
| Result first | A finished expense report fills the screen: "Every receipt. Already filed." (Tallybird) | the output looks satisfying on its own |
| The mess on screen | A grey spreadsheet with forty colour-coded cells: "This is the rota." | the old way looks bad |
| Call the audience | "Run a café? Watch this." | the audience is narrow and easy to name |
| Their own words | Chat bubbles stacking up: "Can anyone swap Saturday?" | the pain has a phrase everyone has heard |
| The site's sharpest line | "Newsletters people finish." (Pageplume's own tagline) | the site already has a line that works as a hook |
| Watch it happen | A running clock while the product does the job: "Next week's rota. Watch." | the product really does the job on camera, at real speed |
| POV | "POV: payroll's tomorrow and the receipts are in your coat." | a default or chaotic tone and a personal, funny pain |

- The hook line is fully on screen at frame 0. Frame 0 is also the poster, and the feed shows it before playback.
  The motion in the first second comes from what's under the line (the mess, the product), not from the line flying in.
- The hook is paid off inside the ad. If it asks a question, the Fix answers it.

Calls to action:

| Pattern | Example | When |
|---|---|---|
| Verb + address | "Try it → shiftnest.app" | the default |
| Outcome + address | "Sundays back. shiftnest.app" | the outcome is the promise |
| Offer + address | "First rota free. shiftnest.app" | only if the site offers exactly that |
| App store | "Tallybird on the App Store", with the badge from the site | only if the site links to an app |

Don't write "link in bio" unless the brief says so. The address on screen still works when the post has no button.

## Copy rules

- Headlines: 2-6 words, at most two lines at the format's headline size, one per scene.
- Second person, present tense, nouns from the product's world: "your rota", "the receipts", never "your workflow".
- **The muted test.** Read the headlines alone, in order. They tell the whole story: pain, cost, fix, proof, address.
- **With a voiceover** (Problem → Solution's default): write the headlines first, then the voice around them. Each
  spoken line and its headline say the same thing (the core voice rule); the voice may add the second half of the
  thought. Say the product's name in the first or second line. Captions are burned in low in the frame after the
  render, so headlines stay inside the format guide's text-safe box, well above them.
- **Music only** (Before / After's default): the headlines carry everything. Cut on strong cues from the track's cue
  file, and land the switch or the reveal on the strongest one.
- Banned, on top of the core list: "Tired of…?", "Say goodbye to", "Meet your new", "Introducing", "the future of",
  "Imagine if", "What if I told you", "you won't believe", "stop scrolling", "hack", "all-in-one", "effortless",
  "#1", "in seconds" (unless the video shows it happen in seconds), and urgency the site doesn't state ("limited
  time", "today only").
- Don't tell viewers what they are: health, money troubles, age, body or beliefs ("Drowning in debt?"). Feed
  platforms reject those ads. Name the situation instead: "Receipts in every pocket?"
- Numbers, quotes and offers come from the site, word for word. If the site has no number, the ad has no number.

## Formats

Vertical first; the beats stay the same in every format.

- **Vertical** (1080×1920): capture the product with `--viewport mobile` and use the vertical guide's "Headline over
  the product" or "Full-bleed product" layout; the phone frame is for a first look, not for reading. If the product
  has no phone layout, a laptop capture at `--scale 3` with the camera on one panel at a time; a whole desktop window
  is unreadable on a phone. Everything the viewer reads or watches happen stays in the text-safe box (x 120-960,
  y 270-1250): feeds lay their buttons along the right edge and their caption and handle along the bottom.
- **Portrait** (1080×1350; Facebook and Instagram feed ads, LinkedIn): the portrait guide's "Headline band and UI"
  or "Full-bleed product" with a mobile capture. The hook and the end card's ask stay above y 965, in case Meta also
  shows the ad in Reels or Stories; the ask matches the ad's button. See [platforms.md](../hyped/formats/platforms.md).
- **Square** (X, LinkedIn): the square guide's "Headline band and UI": the headline above, the camera on one panel
  (or the mobile capture full-bleed) below it.
- **Landscape** (YouTube): the landscape guide's "Split: text left, UI right": the headline in the column, the
  product on the other side.

## Common failures

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Opens on a logo, a title or a fade from black | the hook line and its visual are settled at frame 0 |
| A feature tour | one pain, one fix; cut every scene that doesn't serve the pain |
| The pain drawn as emoji, icons or stock-style art | build the situation in HTML: the spreadsheet, the chat, the inbox. Generic, no real brands |
| Made-up product UI in the Fix | capture the real product with capture.mjs and show it through camera.js |
| A desktop UI shrunk into a vertical frame | the mobile capture, or camera shots on one element at a time |
| Text under the feed's buttons or the captions | the format guide's text-safe box, and its camera inset for the layout |
| The story needs sound to make sense | run the muted test and rewrite the headlines |
| An invented stat in Proof ("Save 5 hours a week") | the site's own number, or the finished result itself |
| Two headlines on screen at once | one line per scene; UI labels stay small and short |
| The address is small or fades before it's read | the format guide's end-card sizes, settled and held 2-3 s |
| A whoosh on every cut | a few effects on the moments that matter (the logo, the key click, the switch), never one per cut |
